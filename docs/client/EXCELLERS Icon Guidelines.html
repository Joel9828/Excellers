Icon Guidelines

EXCELLERS · From Possibilities to Progress. Icons drawn like architecture: a hex mesh for structure, confident lines, dots at the joints, and one Cyan dot where the idea manifests. See the showcase file for the live system.

01

Iconography style & concept

Decision: minimal outlined line icons, built from junctions and dots. Outline, not filled, not duotone. The only solid shapes are dots. Detail is kept low so every icon has room to breathe: the empty space is part of the drawing.

1 · Junction (hex mesh)The hex mesh's open three-way joint becomes the brand's structural unit. Strokes meet at 120°, or run along the mesh angles (0°, 60°, 90°, 120°). Lines may end only on a dot or on another line.
2 · Dot (dot & line)Dots mark joints and ends: a possibility, a decision, an outcome. Each icon has two to five dots. Exactly one is Pacific Cyan, the manifest dot, placed where the idea becomes real.
3 · Draw-up (architecture)Icons are developed like an elevation drawing: lattice, hairline skeleton, final form, then signature dots. Only the final form ships. The hairline weight is reserved for construction and illustration.
4 · Density (graduated line)Lines that thicken into solid blocks express progress and momentum. Used for patterns, loaders and section backgrounds, never inside an icon.
5 · MeshThe open-joint hex mesh is the brand texture. It appears in layouts and illustrations. It never appears as a badge or frame around an icon.

E-Principle cluster

The five stages are shown as a seven-position honeycomb cluster of dots. Unlit positions are hollow ghosts (possibilities). Each stage connects more of them, from one dot to a full network, and the dots added in a stage are Cyan. Use these glyphs for the E-Principle only.

Reserved marks

The horse symbol is the trademark. It is never redrawn as an icon or placed inside the mesh.

Technical specifications

PropertySpecification
Master grid24×24px; 16×16px core; 20×20px live area (2px padding). Exports at 16, 24, 32 and 48.
Construction latticeFlat-top hex mesh, radius 4px, as a visible guide layer only.
AnglesStraight strokes at 0°, 60°, 90° and 120°. No 30° or 45° strokes. Arcs and loops are allowed where the object is round (a circle, an arch).
Stroke2px at 24px; 1.5px at 16px; 2.5px at 32px; 3px at 48px. One weight per icon. Hairline 0.35px is construction-only.
Caps & joinsRound caps and joins. Outer radius 2px on containers.
DotRadius 1.2× stroke (2.4px at 24px). Solid. Two to five per brand icon; UI icons may use one or two.
Manifest dotPacific Cyan, same size as other dots. Exactly one per brand icon, none in UI icons.
Breathing room3px minimum clearance between a dot's edge and any unrelated stroke. Ink should cover no more than about 35% of the live area. Leave at least 8px around an icon.
ContainersCircle or pill only, as a boundary meaning "context". No hexagon badges.
Optical alignmentCentre on visual weight. Round shapes may overshoot the keyline by 1px. Keep dot-to-dot spacing even.
ExportSVG, strokes via currentColor, dots as separate circles with their own class. Outline strokes for print and packaging.

Pattern specifications

DeviceRule
Open-joint meshFlat-top cells, gap of about 20% of the edge at every vertex, stroke 1.5–2.5px, optional dots at a few vertices (one in twenty-five Cyan).
Density gradient45° dash grid, weight growing from 0.5px to near-solid along one diagonal. One direction per layout.

Contrast rules

  • Lines and dots that carry meaning meet 3:1 against the background. Default and inactive states do.
  • Cyan is for the manifest dot only. It is never a line colour on white or Mist (about 2.4:1).
  • The manifest dot never carries meaning alone: state is also shown by line colour or the ring.
  • One-colour use: black, white or Marian, with the manifest dot in the same colour.
  • Status icons use one status colour for lines and dots.
  • Standalone icons need an accessible name; navigation icons need text labels.

05

Do's and don'ts

Do

  • Draw on the lattice at 0°, 60°, 90° and 120°
  • Keep one stroke weight per icon and set
  • Place one Cyan manifest dot at the focal point
  • End every line on a dot or a junction
  • Leave generous empty space around dots
  • Develop new icons through the four draw-up steps
  • Use mesh, density and rhythm for texture and progress

Don't

  • Wrap icons in hexagon badges or frames
  • Use more than five dots, or more than one Cyan dot
  • Leave loose line ends or off-angle strokes
  • Oversize or resize dots independently of the stroke
  • Crowd dots closer than 3px to other strokes
  • Mix stroke weights, add fills, shadows or gradients
  • Use unapproved colours or Cyan lines on white
  • Use density or mesh patterns as icons
  • Redraw the horse symbol as an icon

Visual examples of correct and incorrect use are in the showcase.
